Cylinder wake EDA and PINN benchmark plan

Fixed circular cylinder at Re = 100 · 20 November 2026

Executive summary

Use this run to benchmark sparse reconstruction and hidden-pressure recovery. The supplied field window is a complete stationary-mesh CFD export with 16.66 million node-time records. The separate reaction log confirms a shedding period of 6.066 convective time units, while the field export covers just 3.30 shedding cycles.

Start around the cylinder and its wake. The proposed crop holds 43,855 nodes, 52.9% of the export, in approximately 0.194% of the full-domain area by reconstructed nodal weights. Evaluate the near body and wake separately so that performance reflects the flow features of interest.

Reproduce the method comparison. The uploaded study compares a global PINN with sequential variants on plunging airfoils. Here, compare a global PINN with independently trained time slabs and slabs initialized from earlier weights. Use velocity as the only interior training label and align pressure to a declared gauge before scoring it.

Estimate shedding frequency from the median interval between quadratically refined transverse-force peaks over the late reaction record. There are 60.7 saved field steps per period, so the original field data resolves the shedding fundamental well. Probe spectra come from a short 10-unit window. Their peak locations can shift with windowing, harmonics and phase, and zero padding only refines the frequency grid. For an evenly sampled signal, a 3.03-unit cadence is the nominal Nyquist boundary for the fundamental, and 1.52 units is the boundary for the second harmonic. Stable waveform reconstruction benefits from a finer cadence. Use the reaction log as the measured reference for shedding frequency. Fixed-cylinder PINN specification

Use a smooth network mapping (x, y, t) to (u, v, p). With the dataset scaling and ν* = 0.01, minimize the three residuals below on the fluid region.

rᵤ = uₜ + u uₓ + v uᵧ + pₓ − 0.01 (uₓₓ + uᵧᵧ)
rᵥ = vₜ + u vₓ + v vᵧ + pᵧ − 0.01 (vₓₓ + vᵧᵧ)
r꜀ = uₓ + vᵧ
  • Loss terms. Fit sparse observations of both velocity components, add no-slip velocity on the stationary wall, and add the three physics terms with independently tracked scales. Begin with a tanh model such as 8 layers × 96 units as an engineering starting point. Scale inputs and center time, then differentiate with respect to physical nondimensional coordinates via automatic differentiation through the scaling or explicit chain-rule factors.
  • Actual outer boundary. The companion study documents uniform inlet velocity, homogeneous Neumann velocity at the outlet, symmetry top and bottom, and no slip at the cylinder. Check those against the export before applying them. Use published or verified BCs at the full-domain edges. A crop has artificial outer faces, where you can use a declared budget of measured traces or leave faces unconstrained in a separate sensitivity experiment. Avoid applying the full-domain inlet or outlet conditions at the crop edges. Dataset methods, §3.1
  • Pressure gauge. Momentum uses ∇p, leaving a free spatial constant c(t) at every time. For velocity-only reconstruction, enforce a zero gauge at one declared fluid location or zero mean over a fixed reference set at each time. At evaluation, apply the same operator to predicted and reference pressure. If using a test-frame least-squares offset for a secondary score, disclose it as gauge-aligned shape error. Report pressure differences and drag/lift as gauge-invariant checks. The spatial mean at the exported outlet varies from -1.5e-05 to 5.3e-05. Individual outlet nodes are finite and vary, so treat the mean as evidence about the gauge only.
  • Collocation. Sample inside the fluid, deliberately mixing near-wall annulus, shear layers and wake with broader coverage. Start with approximately 20k–50k geometry-based collocation points per slab, resampled or minibatched as needed, and reserve independent points for residual reporting. Dense collocation in time supplies the PDE only. It does not increase the cadence of labeled velocities.
  • Data scales. Report the data and physics losses separately. Fit any scaling or sampling proxies using allowed training data. Consider component-scaled velocity loss given the larger streamwise mean. Preserve output scaling inside the physics residual. Keep initial full-field labels, outlet pressure labels, and reactions out of the primary velocity-only fit.

How the reference study maps to this project

Sundar, Lucor and Sarkar used a moving-boundary PINN for a periodic plunging foil at Re = 500 and a quasi-periodic case at Re = 300. Velocity training and high-resolution velocity validation use IBM data. The separate ALE reference supplies pressure for testing. Their loss combines sparse velocity, inlet, moving no-slip and fluid-only physics. In our dataset, the wall is stationary and the field and pressure targets share the same Cadyf simulation. Evaluation plan

  1. Freeze the benchmark. Declare the crop, wall mask, seed list, pressure gauge, reference units and purpose of every observation. Use fixed sensor IDs throughout a run. Reserve a spatially stratified set of off-sensor nodes across all times and reserve contiguous spatial patches to distinguish interpolation from filling an uninstrumented region.
  2. Run interpolation first. With 256 sites, observe t = 400, 400.5, …, 420, which gives 10,496 labeled velocity pairs before any boundary labels. Evaluate at held-out sites both at observed and omitted times, with results for each group. Add a separate interior gap, such as withholding every bulk frame with t ∈ [408, 409], with training labels on both sides. Run 64 and 1,024 sites as spatial budget checks and Δtobs = 0.2, 0.5, 1.5 and 3.0 as cadence checks. Include near-wall probes and downstream wake coverage before tightening the budget.
  3. Compare time slabs. Fit a global model, two independently initialized slabs and the same two slabs with transfer initialization. Try [400, 410.5] and [409.5, 420], giving 1.0 of overlap. Use the same label IDs and same boundary access. Count overlapping samples in the compute budget. Match pressure gauges across slabs and measure velocity and pressure jumps at the join. Any overlap consistency objective uses model predictions in the training region only. Keep the evaluation truth entirely outside loss and model selection.
  4. Score temporal extrapolation separately. Train with t ≤ 412, select settings using t ∈ (412, 414), and evaluate once at t ≥ 414. Apply the same restriction to normalization, period fitting, sensor decisions and transfer initialization. If the forecast uses collocation, boundary conditions or an interface in the test horizon, describe it as physics-informed extrapolation and declare that access. The same short wake sequence can test phase prediction within a run. Cross-run or cross-Re generalization needs independent CFD.
  5. Report per-component and regional skill. Give RMSE, MAE, R² and relative L² for u, v and gauge-aligned p. Define relative L² as 100‖prediction − truth‖₂/‖truth‖₂ using declared area/time weights. Also score u fluctuations about the training mean, maximum near-wall velocity error, pressure differences across the cylinder, vorticity from AD, area-weighted divergence and momentum residuals, and the force waveform, mean, amplitude and phase. Include per-time errors and performance across seeds. For shedding-frequency claims, compare a sufficiently long predicted rollout to the reaction reference or report uncertainty from the short window. Record node-weighted and area-weighted results so the weighting choice is visible.
